# Fish Breeder

A Fish Tycoon–style breeding game in Unity with fully 3D fish.

- **Fish never die of old age.** They grow from fry to adult and then live until a chance event gets them.
- **Every meal is a dice roll.** Each pellet a fish eats can make it sick (Aqua Flu, Fin Rot, White Spot, Swim Bladder). Cheap flakes, dirty water, starving fish and low hardiness all raise the odds. Some illnesses spread to nearby fish.
- **Breeding mixes genes.** Every trait has two alleles, one from each parent. Colours and sizes blend; fin shape, tail shape and pattern follow dominant/recessive rules, so rare looks can hide for generations and then pop out.

## Opening the project

1. Install **Unity 6 (6000.0 LTS)** with Unity Hub. Any 6000.0.x patch is fine; Hub may ask to switch versions, say yes.
2. In Unity Hub, **Add > Add project from disk** and pick this folder. First import takes a few minutes.
3. Open any scene (or **File > New Scene**) and press **Play**. `GameBootstrap` builds the store, its three starting tanks, camera, light and HUD automatically.

If Hub refuses to open the folder, create a new **3D (Built-In Render Pipeline)** project called FishBreeder and copy the `Assets` folder from here into it.

## Playing

Each day has three parts. **Morning**: see what happened overnight (births, hatchings, deaths), then feed, clean and fix things. **Shop open**: opening doesn't move time, so nothing changes between your morning routine and the first customer; time passes while the shop is open, and the day's events (births, hatchings, growth, illness) land when it closes. **Evening**: deal with what the day brought, set the tanks up for the night, then **Go to sleep**; time passes again overnight and the next morning starts with a report. Nothing changes while you work in the morning or evening.

**Setup days**: the shop can't open for the first 10 days (`setupDays` in the balance console), so you have time to buy fry and raise something to sell; the grand opening is day 11. Until then the top bar and the register offer **Let the day pass**, which runs the day's time step with no customers and takes you to the evening. After the opening you can still **Stay closed** on any day: time passes and the fish grow, but nothing sells and nobody comes in.

**Grandma**: she believes in you, so every night before the grand opening she sends $100 (`grandmaGift`). After the opening there's no money just for ending the day: the shop has to make a profit. If you go to sleep broke (under $10, `brokeBelow`), she saves the day with another $100.

**Fish journal** (J, or the Journal button): every species in the game, grouped into cold fresh water, tropical fresh water and salt water. Species you haven't found yet are dark silhouettes with a ?, and say which Fishkeeping level brings them. The first time you see a species for what it is (a known fish arriving, or a mystery fry growing enough to tell), a "New fish discovered!" card pops up and the journal opens on it. Click any found fish for its profile: photo, latin name, family, rarity, where it lives, how many you have, and everything research has taught you about it.

**Milestones**: one-off goals that pay cash and XP and get you your first real money before the opening (first fish, first feed and wipe, a fish growing up, first equipment, hatching, breeding, discovering 3 and 6 species, keeping 10 fish, the grand opening, first sale, and more). The next three are listed on the right of the store screen with progress bars; reaching one shows a celebration card. They're set in the balance console under Levels & XP.

**Dev mode** (F10, in the editor and development builds): everything is unlocked whatever your levels and buying is free, so you can try anything; the top bar shows DEV MODE instead of your money. It stays on between plays until you press F10 again.

**Watch mode** (V, or the Watch button): hides every menu so you can just watch the aquarium; the camera drifts gently. V or Esc brings the menus back, and you can still click fish to follow them.

**Look and feel**: an ocean and tropical theme throughout (deep-sea blue panels, turquoise buttons, coral and sunny highlights, a beach-shack shop with wooden floors, palms and surfboards). Things that happen get a little animation: bubbles and ripples when you feed, sparkles when you wipe, hearts when fish pair up or eat a favourite, "+3 fry!", "Hatched!", "All grown up!", "CHOMP!", confetti and coin bursts on sales and milestones, an OPEN! stamp and a sunrise wash at each new day.

**The shop's people**: the first time you play you pick who runs the shop, a woman or a man (switch any time from the register). The owner stands behind the till, waves at people coming in, watches whoever is nearest, hands over the bag with a thumbs up or a clap, and wipes the counter, stretches or looks around when it's quiet. Four kinds of customer walk the shop with a proper walk; at a tank their eyes follow the fish, and they react to what they see: pointing at a fish, turning back and going wow, leaning in for a closer look, thinking, nodding, shaking their head, looking grossed out by a dirty tank or getting mad. They cheer after buying and sometimes wave goodbye. The fish seller (the big guy in the Hawaiian shirt) parks his wholesale van on the pavement outside, west of the door, with its back doors open, a table of aerated holding tanks and bagged fish and a stack of foam shipping boxes; he stands behind the table with his arms folded, beckons you over while the shop is shut, waves when you walk up and shows off his stock when you buy.

**Fry don't fight**: only adults hunt, nip or scare other fish, and a species only makes others nervous once it has adults in the tank. Fry can still be eaten by a predator.

**Shop closed** (you start here with $1,000, a tub of flakes and three empty tanks: For Sale and Display along the shop's back wall, the Breeder tank in the breeding room to the west, which customers never see). Tanks stand round the walls facing into the room: on the main floor along the west wall and the back wall, in the breeding room along its west, back and east walls, in the other sections along the back and front walls, which leaves the middle free. The breeding room also has the supplier computer by its front wall and a little back door onto an empty parking lot, where the fish seller parks his van (a sign over the door slides the view there). The **East wing** keeps its whole back wall for the **wall aquarium** ($12,000, Shopkeeping 8, `store.wallAquarium` in the console): one tank 12 m long, 1.95 m tall and 1.33 m deep, set into the wall and reaching out behind it, holding 200 fish and 48 decorations (four grand tanks' worth), as a display or for sale tank. Its other two tanks stand side on at the room's edges, so nothing blocks the view of it. The register stands side-on along the east wall by the door, so the shopkeeper faces into the shop and customers queue back into the room.

- **Store view**: A/D or left/right arrows pan along the shop, W/S or up/down arrows slide back to the parking lot and forward to the street (right-drag does both), scroll to zoom. Everything clickable has a label.
- **People camera** (C): follows the shopkeeper, then the fish seller, then each customer up close; Esc goes back to the store view. In the editor, G plays each gesture in turn on whoever you follow and H toggles slow motion (shop closed only).
- **Levels**: three tracks up to level 60, shown in the top bar with today's goals. Fishkeeping XP comes from feeding, wiping, curing, hatching and breeding (more for rarer fish and looks) and unlocks equipment, food, egg boxes, rarer species, tank sizes, breeder and quarantine tanks. Shopkeeping XP comes from sales, happy customers, good reviews and buying things for the shop, and unlocks decorations and more display and for sale tanks. Research XP comes from the fish you keep, bounties and finished research, and brings faster research and new bounties. Each day has one goal per track worth a big chunk of XP. Higher Fishkeeping makes wipes clean more and adds an extra wipe every 8 levels.
- **Perks** (P, or the pulsing perk point button in the top bar): every Fishkeeping and Shopkeeping level gives a perk point. Fishkeeping points go into the **Aquariums** (cleaner water, stronger wipes, slower algae, more decoration spots, sturdier equipment, roomier tanks, an extra wipe), **Biology** (fewer illnesses, slower hunger, wider temperature and pH tolerance, gentler medicine, faster growth, longer life, faster research) or **Breeding** (breeding chance, shorter rests, bigger litters, hardy eggs, quicker births, more mutations, more cross-breeding, pricier home-bred fish) tree. Shopkeeping points go into **Finances** (supplier discounts, customers paying more, tips, bigger gifts from Grandma, more sales, interest on savings), **Construction** (cheaper tanks, rooms, upgrades and decorations, more shop tanks, more traffic from tanks and decorations, room for more customers) or **Public Relations** (more customers, reputation that rises faster and falls slower, customers forgiving dirty tanks and dead fish, longer browsing, nightly reputation). Each tree has four tiers; a tier opens once 5 points are spent in the tiers above it. Perks have several ranks. Every perk's numbers, and the points per level and per tier, are on the console's Perks tab.
- **Sick fish** stand out: a pulsing red cross over each sick fish in the tank, a red "+ SICK" badge and illness card in its window, a sick list in the tank panel and a sick count on the tank's label in the store. Each illness looks like the real thing, a little cartoony: white spot covers the fish in white grains, fin rot leaves ragged pale fin edges with red sores at the base, swim bladder makes it list on its side, and aqua flu makes it pale, shivery and sneezing bubbles.
- **Fish seller and supplier computer** (tabs, each item with a picture: photos of the real decoration, equipment and fish models, drawn icons for the rest): egg and fry boxes are bought from the **fish seller** at his van out front (he goes home in the evening, so his buttons grey out with "Back in the morning" until the next day); decorations, backgrounds and food stay on the **supplier computer**. Decorations and backgrounds go into your **stock** when bought (the computer shows how many you have in stock and in aquariums), and you put them into a tank from that tank's Tank tab, so three plants can go one in one tank and two in another. Equipment is bought from each tank's **Upgrades** tab instead. **Water treatment** (conditioner: resets the pH and clears snails and worms) is bought per tank, straight from the tank's Care tab ($5 by default, `supplier.waterTreatmentPrice`). Things above your level show what unlocks them. **Fry boxes** work like egg boxes but can hold any species (livebearers only come this way, egg boxes only hold egg-layers). Bought fry are all tiny grey fish, so you can't tell what they are until they grow into juveniles; fry you breed yourself are known. **Equipment** comes in four quality tiers per kind for the normal tanks (filters: sponge, hang-on-back, canister, sump; heaters: basic, adjustable, titanium, smart controller; bubblers: air stone, twin-outlet, silent pump, oxygen reactor). The wall aquarium takes none of those: it has its own two per kind (a plant-room sump and a drum filter with UV, twin inline heaters and a heat exchanger, an air blower and an oxygen injection system), set by `wallAquariumOnly` in the console, so later big tanks can get their own gear the same way. Better tiers clean more, hold the temperature closer to the setting, or give air to more fish, and break down less. Equipment wears with age and can break at any time step (the chance grows with wear and is shown in the tank panel); a broken filter, heater or bubbler does nothing until you **pay to repair it**. Each breakdown says what went wrong ("The sponge needed replacing", "The circuit got wet") and the price is that model's repair cost times the fault's, give or take 15%: better models break less but cost more to fix. Each repair only takes off a quarter of the wear, so old kit keeps breaking. Egg boxes hold mixed species: **Simple** eggs hatch common fish with a small chance of a rare one; **Rare** eggs hatch a mix with a good chance of rare fish. Every rare egg already in a box makes the next one less likely, so 2 rares in a box is much harder than 1 (the odds are listed). **Wild tetra eggs** open once you've raised 50 neon tetras or tiger barbs to adulthood (any mix; the box shows your progress, and a card pops up when it opens). They hold 17 tetras you can't get anywhere else: bloodfin, serpae, X-ray, black skirt, redeye, lemon, bleeding heart, Buenos Aires, emperor, flame, Congo, penguin, glowlight, bucktooth, ruby, silvertip and rummy nose, with the Congo, ruby and bucktooth as the rare finds. Any box can have a goal like this (`raiseSpecies` and `raiseCount` in the console), and a species with `onlyInBox` set only ever comes in that box. Shoppers don't ask for wild tetras until the box is open. Ultra Rare, Legendary and Ancient boxes are locked for later. **Backgrounds** (supplier computer, end of the Decorations tab): six pictures for a tank's back glass (sandy shallows and open ocean $15, planted stream and driftwood forest $20, coral reef $25, shipwreck $30, opening with Shopkeeping levels 1 to 4). Each goes into stock, is put up in a tank from its Tank tab, cropped to fit its size, tinted green as the water gets dirty, and adds aquarium quality; taking it down puts it back in stock and leaves plain glass. Prices, levels and names are under `supplier.backgrounds`; the pictures are `Resources/Backgrounds/<id>.jpg`.
- **Click a tank** to look inside. The panel's header shows the aquarium's **level** as a number in a circle on every tab (coral when a fish in it wants a higher level); hover it or the **?** beside it (click **?** to keep it open) for a little box beside the panel with the quality score, what each level needs and which fish want more. Its tabs (a coral dot marks one that needs you): **Care** (the daily jobs first: feeding with any food you own, wiping (1 wipe per tank per session, more with Fishkeeping levels), treating the water, dosing the whole tank with medicine, repairing broken equipment, removing dead fish, the heater; then the tank's specs: the fish count against its limit, sick fish, eggs and pregnancies, water quality, algae, oxygen, temperature, pH once you can test it, salt, pests), **Tank** (the next size up, fresh or salt water, **Decorations**: a button that opens a picker beside the panel with the decorations in stock (pictures, how many, which fish here like each one) to put in, and the ones in this tank to take out back to stock; **Move** beside it: a top view where you drag each piece where you want it, Q/E or the mouse wheel turn it, and nothing can go past the glass (untouched pieces keep their automatic spots, and a button puts them all back); **Background**: the same kind of picker to put one up from stock, swap it or take it down, and **Adjust** beside it: drag it to slide it and zoom with the wheel or slider, never smaller than the glass; and the **tank type**: turn a for sale tank into a display tank or back, or a breeder into quarantine, for $100, `store.changeTankTypePrice`), **Upgrades** (filter, heater and bubbler: each shows what's fitted, or "No heater" with what that risks; click it to see every model for this tank, the ones above your level greyed out), **Breeding** (eggs, pregnancies, who's ready, and with the Stock book perk a list of every fish by kind, sex and age that you can pick fish from) and, in the editor or dev mode, **Dev** (clean or dirty water, any equipment model or none, any decoration in or out, and any fish: an adult, a male and female pair or five fry, all free). Click empty water to zoom in on that spot, as close as following a fish; once zoomed in, WASD or the arrow keys slide the view around the tank (WASD zooms in from the whole-tank view too, and while following a fish lets go of it); Esc or a right click zooms back out (a right click in a tank goes back to the store). A sick fish can be euthanized from its window (it asks first). Food still sinking when time passes is eaten by the hungry fish (the one chasing it first) in the time you don't see. With a heater you can set the temperature (it shows the range every fish in the tank is happy with, as far as research knows). The temperature is shown as a hot-to-cold bar with the liked range outlined. The tank floor is gravel in fresh water and sand with bits of coral in salt water. **Tank limit**: a tank can hold up to 1.5x its limit, but every fish over it dirties the water faster, spreads illness and stresses everyone. Orders from the supplier only fill a tank to its limit.
- **Aquarium quality**: every tank has a quality score from its equipment (more for better models, half for broken ones) and decorations (extra for plants and for each different kind). The tank's **level** is 1, then 2 at the quality for Large (25) and 3 at the quality for Grand (55); reaching it lets you upgrade the tank to that size for money. Bigger tanks keep fish that want more room: angelfish, silver dollars, red-tailed sharks, green terrors and blue acaras need a Large tank and the oscar a Grand one (each species' smallest tank is in the console). The fish seller only puts fish in a box that fit the tank it's for (the shop lists which fish are waiting on bigger tanks), a fish won't move into a tank that's too small, and one that ends up cramped is less comfortable. A fish also needs the tank's level to stay at its size's (level 2 for a Large-tank fish): take out enough decorations or downgrade the equipment so the level drops and those fish get unhappy, the more so the further the quality falls short (`health.lowLevelComfortLoss`, `lowLevelLossPerPoint`, `lowLevelMaxLoss`). Taking something out or fitting a lesser model warns you first if it would do that.
- **Favourite decorations and roamers**: many species have favourite decorations (angelfish and neon tetras tall plants and leafy plants, corydoras driftwood, the cichlids rocks, clownfish seaweed and the clam shell, most wild tetras plants). With one in the tank they potter and nose about near it rather than in open water, and are a little happier (`health.likedDecorComfort`). Roamers (goldfish, guppies, barbs, danios, silver dollars, mollies and a few tetras) swim long laps round the whole tank instead. Both are set per species on the console's Habits card.
- **Aggression is about distance**: fish that would fight or hunt only do it when they meet. Each tank measures how much of the time each pair spends within fight range (0.45 m) or hunt range (0.7 m) of each other; at 30% of the time or more they fight and hunt at full chance, at 6% or less never, and in between it scales. In a small tank that's always, in the wall aquarium rarely, so rivals can share a big enough tank. Fear of a predator scales the same way. The ranges and shares are under `tankMates` in the console.
- **Food**: flakes, algae wafers (they sink fast, for bottom feeders), premium pellets, frozen brine shrimp and frozen bloodworms. Every fish eats any food, but each species has favourites that fill it more and cheer it up. Feeding a favourite sometimes reveals it.
- **Research** (R, or the Research button): the bounty board posts tasks like feed a tank 40 times, wipe a tank 30 times, breed 30 fry or cure 8 sick fish (targets are in the console's Research tab). Finish one and its research project is ready; start it at the research bench (one at a time; switching to another keeps the first one's points), and queue up to 3 more to start by themselves, with the night's spare points carried over. Like Civilization, each project costs research points and the bench earns points every night: 10, plus 0.2 for every dollar of your **daily research budget** (set it with +/- or the quick buttons; it's paid each night while something is at the bench, never while it's empty, and never so much it leaves you broke), all times your research speed (higher with Research levels). Finished research makes the game tell you more: health check-ups show how much each thing adds or takes from a fish's health, temperature and pH ranges, exactly who a fish fears, fights or likes, the water testing kit (pH), favourite foods, why a fish isn't ready to breed, shoal sizes, how far an illness has gone and which medicine cures it, plants and lifespan. Higher Research levels post new bounties. Until then the game just says less, e.g. "Squabbles with a tank mate" instead of who with.
- **Click a fish** for its status window (it scrolls): sex, age and size (every fish is small, medium or large for its kind, just for looks) as coloured badges, health and where it's heading at the next time step, what is pulling it down (hunger, illness, comfort, water, crowding, old age, dead tank mates) with each cause on its own line, its needs as far as research knows (temperature and pH bars), what it thinks of its tank mates, appraised value, price (For Sale tanks; only juveniles and adults sell) and moving it to another tank, after which the next fish is picked. The camera follows it zoomed in; the left/right arrow keys or the < > buttons beside it step through the tank's fish. Esc closes the fish, Esc again leaves the tank. With no fish selected, the arrow keys or the < > bar at the bottom move to the previous or next tank (back room first, then the shop floor left to right).
- **Medicine** goes into the whole tank: it cures every fish with the matching illness, but stresses healthy fish (lower comfort for a few time steps) and builds up in every fish. Too much in a short time is an overdose that permanently lowers health and value, and two overdoses leave a fish unable to breed.
- **Tank mates**: every pair of species has a relationship, set in the balance console from real fishkeeping advice. **Predator**: the fish hunts the other, eating it if it is small enough to fit in its mouth and biting it otherwise (an oscar eats neons, an angelfish eats neons and guppies, goldfish eat neons). **Competitive**: they stress each other, and in an overcrowded tank they nip and injure each other (tiger barbs and long-finned fish, two male bettas, mbuna with most fish). **Preferred**: both are happier and a little healthier together (schooling fish with their own kind, neons with corydoras, fantails with danios). **Neutral**: they get along fine.
- **Evening and morning**: the shop lists what needs doing, always in the same order: hungry fish, sick fish, dead fish, dirty tanks, then broken equipment, and takes you to each one in turn, moving on by itself once it's dealt with. You can skip, go back or stop and pick it up later from the top bar.
- **Breeding**: any compatible pair (an adult male and female of the same species, healthy, fed, rested, and happy with the water) may breed at each time step, likeliest in a breeder tank; in a breeder tank you can also pair two fish by hand. Livebearers (guppy, platy, black molly) get pregnant and give birth a few time steps later; egg-layers lay a clutch on the sand that hatches a few time steps later. Filthy water kills eggs. The tank panel lists eggs and pregnant fish with how long until they hatch or give birth. Pregnancy length and hatch time are per species in the balance console.
- **Cross-breeding**: some species can breed with each other (set on the console's Cross-breeding tab; by default comet × fantail goldfish, guppy × black molly, and green terror × blue acara, all real hybrids). A fish with no partner of its own kind may cross with a compatible one at each time step, at half the usual chance (`crossChanceMultiplier`). Their fry get the **body of one parent and the fins of the other**; which way round is rolled from the two looks' chances, and the tail follows the fins three times in four (`tailWithFins`). The body decides everything else (needs, behaviour, how it's born), and the mother decides whether the young come as fry or eggs. Hybrids breeding on pass each part down from either parent, but only into looks a compatible pair could make. Colour, pattern, fin and tail shape, size and hardiness are still inherited and mutate as usual. Every look has its own name and price in the console (e.g. "Guppy with Black Molly fins"), and that price replaces the species' value, so a rare combination can be worth a lot. The supplier's dev tab has a **Random mix** button that gives you an adult with a random body, fins and tail from any species, to see that every fin fits every body.
- **Empty tank spots** and **locked sections**: buy more tanks and floor space (rooms need enough stars, see Reputation).

**Open Shop** (top bar or register, from day 11): customers come in for one real hour (no time step runs on opening). They wander between tanks, react with thought bubbles (happy, grossed out by bad water or sick fish, sad about dead fish or fish fighting, angry about prices), sometimes buy a juvenile or adult from a For Sale tank (a dead fish in the shop makes some sad and puts others off a little), pay at the register and go home. The shop closes on its own, the day's time step runs, and you get the day's report and reviews (many different ones for each complaint), which move your reputation and next day's traffic. In the evening the shop goes dark blue and dim; morning brings the daylight back.

**Reputation** (Y, or click the stars in the top bar): your stars, where foot traffic comes from (display tanks, other tanks, decorations, stars, perks), and what shoppers have been complaining about lately, each with what it costs: dead fish, dirty water and not enough fish for sale cut foot traffic; high prices, sick fish and fish fighting cut sales (e.g. "Prices too high: -8% sales"). Complaints are remembered for a few days and fade. It also lists the latest reviews and the five **expansion rooms** (East wing, Garden room, Coral lounge, Lagoon hall, Grand hall), which open in order at 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3 and 3.5 stars, with their prices. The shop starts at half a star and stars come slowly: a day only raises reputation when more than 60% of shoppers' reactions are good (sales count twice), each star is harder than the last, a bad day costs about twice a good one and quiet days count less (the console's Reputation tab shows how many days each star takes). Shoppers who leave without their favourite kind because none was for sale show up as wishes ("Wish they had more corydoras catfish") in the reviews and on this screen. Sick fish put shoppers off (more of them the more sick fish they see) and never sell.

**Attractiveness**: every fish has a 0-100 rating (shown with stars in its window) from its looks (fancier fins, tails and patterns), how rare its kind is, its size, its age, its health and a bit of its own charm. Eye-catching fish on the shop floor bring more people in, shoppers look at them first, and they sell more easily.

**Journal prices**: each fish's journal page shows what one is appraised at (adult, juvenile, fry) and lists every one you own with its appraised value and price; fish in For Sale tanks can be priced there one by one or all at once at a percentage of appraised.

**Evening list**: after feeding, sickness, dead fish, dirty water and broken equipment, the last suggestions are tanks more than 10% dirty that could use a wipe.

**Finances** (F, or click your money): money in and out today and yesterday; spending by category (Shopkeeping level 2), income by category (after your first sale), a day-by-day profit chart (Shopkeeping level 3), earnings per tank (after $1,000 of sales) and best-selling fish (the Bookkeeper perk). Each part says what opens it. Developer buttons in the top bar speed the day up (5x, 20x) or skip the rest of it; the keys 1, 2, 3 and K do the same.

Each time step: eggs develop and hatch when due, pregnant fish give birth when due; water quality drops with waste, algae grows (faster with more decorations), pH drifts and pests may appear; fish age and get hungrier, and their health moves toward what their conditions allow (it only changes at time steps); illness progresses, spreads or strikes; equipment wears and may break; predators hunt and crowded rivals nip (while the shop is open you see predators dart at their prey now and then); compatible pairs may breed (likelier in breeder tanks); and fish in poor health roll for death.

**Text never runs off a panel**: every window and scroll list goes through `UiCheck`, which measures what its text needs each frame (windows grow to fit, scroll lists keep their text clear of the scrollbar, the top bars take two rows in a narrow window, and windows shrink to fit a small screen). Anything that still doesn't fit, or a window that runs off the screen, is logged as a `[UI overflow]` warning naming the window. In the editor, **F8** runs the UI check: it opens every window, supplier tab, report, tank tab and the fish window in turn and reports how many were checked and what overflowed. It checks at the current window size, so try it with the Game view at a few sizes. **F7** (in the editor or dev mode) opens the **menu layout editor**: every window gets a pink outline and name tag, and every piece inside them (each button, label, toggle, slider, bar and picture, drawn through `Ui` in `Ui.cs`) a teal one, named after its window and text. Drag a tag (or Ctrl-drag inside a window) to move it, drag its corner to resize it, click a piece to pick it (buttons still click, so tabs can be opened while editing) and drag it to move it (Shift-drag resizes), and right-click to reset. Drags snap to the edges and middles of other windows (pieces: other pieces in the same window), with a pink guide line, or else to an 8 px grid; hold Alt to drag freely. The arrow keys nudge the last thing touched by 1 px (Shift: 8 px, Ctrl: resize). A picked label, button or toggle can be retyped in the bar's text box (`{text}` stands for what the game would say there, so a changing number can be kept) or hidden with Delete or the Hide button: hidden pieces get a pink outline and show faintly while editing, and take no room once the editor closes. Right-click puts a piece fully back. The bar at the bottom turns snapping, the grid and piece outlines on and off and shows the last thing's position and size. The nudges are saved to `Assets/Resources/UiLayout.json` as you go, so builds keep them.

## Balance console

All balancing numbers (species rarity, prices, needs, relationships between species, diseases, customers, time-step chances, health weights, store and supplier prices, egg boxes and their hatch tables (the **Egg hatching** tab: every fish each box can hatch, its chance and the level and day it can first turn up, with the odds worked out for any level and day), decorations, tank sizes, unlock levels, XP and daily goals, closing notifications) live in one file, `Assets/Resources/Balance/balance.json`. The search box at the top finds any setting by name or help text. Edit them in the browser: with the project open in Unity, choose **Fish Breeder > Balance Console** (or go to http://localhost:8642). **Save** writes the file and, if the game is playing, applies the new values straight away. The console is an editor-only tool (`Tools/BalanceConsole/index.html`, served by `Assets/Scripts/Editor/BalanceConsoleServer.cs`) and is not part of a build; the JSON file is, so the game ships with whatever you saved. Until you first save, the defaults in code are used. Click any table heading to sort by it (again to flip). Numbers are kept to 2 decimals. If a save can't reach Unity (for example while it recompiles), the console keeps retrying for a minute and keeps your edits in the browser until one gets through. Species added to the game later join your saved file automatically with their default values.

**Pinned**: click the ☆ next to any setting to pin it; pinned settings collect on the **Pinned** tab (the first tab, and the one that opens when you have pins), grouped by where they live. Click ★ to unpin. Pins are kept in your browser, not in the balance file.

**Swimming tab**: every fish laid out side-on with a measuring line from nose to tail. Drag the line to where its body starts to wiggle (`bendStart`): everything in front of it stays stiff and the wiggle grows to full strength over the next 30% of the body, so danios and tetras snake from just behind the head while goldfish, angelfish and cichlids keep a stiff body and swing the back. A top view under each fish swims with the setting, using the same wave as the game. The pictures are made by `Tools/Meshy/console_pictures.py <project root>` (rerun it after adding fish models).

**Simulator**: plays out days of tank keeping with the console's current numbers (unsaved edits included) and no game. Set a starting budget, how many days, how many runs to average, and your Fishkeeping level; then add tanks, each with its kind, size, salt, decorations, filter, heater and bubbler, a daily routine (feedings and food, wipes, removing dead fish, medicine, water treatment, repairs, breeding on or off, selling grown fish above a number) and the fish in it. **Run** shows how many fish are alive, born and dead, how many fell ill, were cured or got better alone, what medicine cost, the money left and whether (and when) you'd have gone broke, with charts of fish, money, sick fish and each tank's water over time (the line is the average, the band the best and worst run), plus per-species counts, spending, causes of death and a diary of the first run. Untick Grandma's bailouts to see when you'd really go broke. It's a copy of the game's time-step rules in the console's JavaScript, so a rule changed in C# needs the same change there. Perks, research, customers and milestones are left out. The setup is kept in your browser.

## Fish models

The first real fish is the **Comet Goldfish** in `Assets/Resources/Fish/CometGoldfish` (Meshy model cut from 33,936 to 3,000 triangles, 1024 textures). Every fish uses it for now. It swims with the `FishBreeder/FishSwim` shader driven by `SwimSpine`: the body is laid along the path the head has just travelled (so turns curve the whole fish), carries a carangiform body wave using the measured mackerel amplitude envelope (about 2% of body length at the nose, 10% at the tail tip, one wave per body), and rolls into turns with a small tail twist. Tail beats speed up when the fish accelerates or turns and relax into gentle strokes while gliding, and headings ease in and out with a damped spring. The head end is detected automatically, so any Meshy fish exported with Y up works. Fish steer around decorations (rocks, the rock arch, driftwood, plants), keep off the glass, and pick wander targets in open water. Anything with a collider and a `TankObstacle` component counts as an obstacle.

To add another Meshy fish, see `Docs/MeshyPipeline.md`.

**Split models.** Every fish model is split into objects named `Body`, `Dorsal` (back fins), `Ventral` (belly fins), `Pectoral` (side fins, where the fish has them) and `Tail`, plus `Fill_<Part>` patches that close the body where each part was cut off (the angelfish, betta and oscar close their tail cut with their dorsal and ventral patches). Multi-fish Meshy collages are first cut into one FBX per fish by `Tools/Meshy/split_fish_kit.py <kit> <collage.fbx> <out folder>` (kits listed in its `KITS` table), which also straightens tails Meshy bent sideways, moves detached fins onto the body and decimates each fish (the wild tetras are about 2,000 triangles each, in `Resources/Fish/TetraSetA` and `TetraSetB`). `Tools/Meshy/split_fins.py` made them from the original single-mesh models with Blender's Python module (`pip install bpy`, then `python3 split_fins.py <folder of unsplit FBXs> <output folder>`): fins are found by how thin the mesh is, the tail is cut at the caudal peduncle, and per-species fixes are in its `OVERRIDES` table. In game, `FishParts` fits another species' parts onto a body by where they sat along their own body: dorsal fins follow the new back, ventral fins the new belly, side fins keep their height on the flank, and the tail is fixed to the peduncle, all scaled to the new body. A pure fish uses its own parts and looks exactly like the whole model.

## Equipment

The filters, heaters and bubblers are in `Assets/Resources/Equipment` (each piece 600 to 2,400 triangles with a low-poly `_Collider` hull fish swim around, and 1024 textures).

**Filters** are three hang-on-back filters from a Blender file of Kristian's, cut with `Tools/Meshy/split_hangon_filters.py` (`python3 split_hangon_filters.py -- <filters.blend> <output folder>`, Blender's Python module): each is one piece whose origin is put where it hangs, on the glass at the top edge, with +Y towards the outside. In the tank one hangs on the right-hand glass towards the back, the way a real one does: the intake tube reaches down inside, the box with the media sits outside the glass. `TankFilterFlow` shows the water it moves: specks are drawn into the tube's mouth at the bottom, and a stream pours back in over the rim, splashing into the surface. A broken filter stops both. The filters have their own `Filters_` textures.

**Heaters and bubblers** come from a Meshy sheet cut with `Tools/Meshy/split_equipment.py` (`-- <sheet.fbx> <output folder> [equipment|filters]`; its `PLAN` tables find each piece by where it sits on the sheet) and share the `Equipment_` textures. That sheet's own three filters are kept as `FilterBase0-2` for the part that will go under the tank, not used yet. Heaters stand upright against the back glass on the left; the smart controller's control box hangs on the outside of the left glass with a cable over the rim. Bubblers sit on the sand with the bubbles coming off their top.

There are four models of each kind and three designs, so two neighbouring models share one: filters 0/1/1/2, heaters 0/1/2/2, bubblers 0/1/1/2 (the table is in `EquipmentModels`). Changing a tank's model swaps the design straight away. In the editor, **F9** in a tank view installs the next model of all three kinds, to look at each design.

## Characters

The owner (woman and man), four customers and the fish seller are in `Assets/Resources/Crew`, cut from one Meshy lineup model (about 7,000 triangles each, sharing one 2048 texture). There are no animation clips: each has a simple skeleton, and `CrewRig` moves it by code (breathing, glances, a walk cycle, the head and body turning to look at things, and keyframed gestures like PointAndWow, Wow, Think, Wave, Clap, HandOver and Beckon). To replace them after fixing the meshes, run the three scripts in `Tools/Meshy/crew` with Blender's Python module: `crew_split.py -- <lineup.fbx>` splits and names the characters (left to right as in the lineup), `crew_rig.py` finds the joints, cuts each mesh to 7,000 triangles and skins it, and `crew_export.py` writes `out/<Name>.fbx`. Copy those over the files in `Assets/Resources/Crew` (and the lineup's base colour and normal textures to `Crew_Albedo.png` and `Crew_Normal.png`). Meshes must be in a T-pose facing forward.
